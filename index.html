How to read this card

The market. A Kalshi YES contract pays $1 if weekly initial claims come in at or above the threshold. Its fair price in cents equals the probability of that happening.

The models. Each turns a view of this week's print into that probability: P(claims ≥ T) = Φ((center − T) ÷ σ). No-change repeats last week's print (σ 13.0K). Consensus blend moves halfway to the economist consensus (σ 12.4K). Forecast blend moves 90% of the way to the in-house forecast (σ 12.0K). A model only participates when its input is filled in; the card never invents a consensus or forecast.

The bars. Each bar is a model's fair YES price on a 0–100¢ track. The faint mark is 50¢; the dark tick is the market price. Fill reaching past the tick means that model calls YES cheap.

The bell. The curve shows where the selected model expects the print to land. The shaded slice at or above the threshold is the fair YES price — the share of outcomes that pay out. Tap a model row to switch curves. The σ chips under it let you slide the threshold and watch the tail shrink: threshold − center = 1σ leaves 16¢, 2σ leaves 2.3¢.

Fees. Taker (market) orders pay C·r·p(1−p) where C is contract count and r=1.75%; max is about 0.44¢ at p=50¢. Maker (resting) orders pay one quarter of that, max 0.11¢. Edges under 1¢ rarely survive.

Caution. These models missed every 15K+ weekly move in the test sample. If the market prices a big move, it may know something this card doesn't.
